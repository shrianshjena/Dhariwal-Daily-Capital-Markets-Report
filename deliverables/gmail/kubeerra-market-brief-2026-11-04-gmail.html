The morning in brief

 
The NIFTY 50 rose 1.58% to 23,810.85 and the Sensex 1.62% to 76,917.85 on Tuesday, a fourth straight gain, led by FMCG (up 3.65%) and private banks (up 1.98%), while PSU banks slipped 1.27%.
 
Foreign institutions bought a net ₹6,837 crore and domestic institutions sold ₹3,679 crore (provisional). The rupee rose 9 paise to 94.75 per dollar, and the 10-year government bond yield eased to about 7.30%.
 
Brent crude settled 1.12% lower at $103.60 a barrel as US-Iran talks continued. On Wall Street, on US midterm election day, the Nasdaq rose 0.83% and the Dow fell 0.38%.

04 · TOP STORIES

What moved, and why it matters for Indian investors

FLOWS · MARKET-WIDE

Foreign investors bought a net ₹6,837 crore of Indian shares on Tuesday, their largest single day in about a year

Provisional exchange data showed foreign institutional investors as net buyers of ₹6,836.81 crore in the cash market on 3 November, a third straight day of net buying, while domestic institutions were net sellers of ₹3,678.85 crore. Business Standard said it was the largest single-day foreign purchase since October 2025.

WHY IT MATTERS  Foreign portfolio flows move the broad indices and the rupee: PTI linked the rupee's 9-paise gain on Tuesday partly to these inflows. Provisional figures can be revised when NSDL publishes final data.

Sources: Business Standard, Trendlyne (exchange data), PTI via ThePrint

 

CORPORATE · MARKET-WIDE

State Bank of India's September-quarter net profit rose 14.3% to ₹23,038 crore as bad loans eased

SBI reported on 3 November a standalone net profit of ₹23,038 crore for July to September, 14.3% more than a year earlier. Gross non-performing assets (loans in default) eased to 1.39% of loans, and net NPAs to 0.36%. Advances grew nearly 18%. The shares closed 1.77% lower at ₹969.55 on BSE that day.

WHY IT MATTERS  SBI is India's largest lender and carries large weights in the Nifty Bank and PSU bank indices, so its loan growth and asset quality are read as a gauge of credit demand and bank balance sheets.

Sources: Business Standard, HDFC Sky

 

GLOBAL · MARKET-WIDE

Brent settled 1.12% lower at $103.60 as the US and Iran remained apart on a deal, with Qatar mediating

Reuters reported on 3 November that an Iranian official called the US response to Iran's conditions unconvincing, while Qatar said both sides were reviewing its proposals. Brent rose about 1% after reports of overnight US strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island, then settled at $103.60 a barrel, its lowest settlement since 7 October.

WHY IT MATTERS  India imports most of its crude oil, so oil prices pass through to the import bill, the rupee, fuel costs and inflation.

Sources: Reuters via The Korea Times, The Nation (Thailand), HDFC Sky

 

GLOBAL · MARKET-WIDE

Wall Street ended mixed on US midterm election day as chip shares recovered

On 3 November the S&P 500 rose 0.36% and the Nasdaq Composite 0.83% as semiconductor shares rebounded, while the Dow fell 0.38%. Reuters reported early in Asian hours on 4 November that US stock futures were lower while election results were still coming in; control of Congress had not been confirmed.

WHY IT MATTERS  The make-up of the US Congress shapes US fiscal, trade and technology policy, which reaches India through foreign portfolio flows, the dollar and US bond yields, and through demand for Indian IT services.

Sources: Reuters via Investing.com, Yahoo Finance, MT Newswires via MarketScreener

 

CORPORATE · SECTOR

NSE's September-quarter net profit rose 23% from a year earlier to ₹2,578 crore, in its first results as a listed company

National Stock Exchange reported on 3 November a consolidated net profit of ₹2,578 crore for July to September, against ₹2,098.5 crore a year earlier but 17% below the June quarter. Revenue from operations rose 10.6% to ₹4,066.8 crore, while transaction charges fell 16% from the previous quarter to ₹3,035 crore.

WHY IT MATTERS  Transaction charges, earned on trading in cash shares and derivatives, made up about three quarters of NSE's revenue from operations this quarter, so its results move with how actively investors trade.

Sources: Business Standard, Business Today

08 · LEARN · L-005

What a fund of funds is, and why it has two layers of costs

A fund of funds does not invest in shares or bonds directly. It invests in other mutual fund schemes or ETFs, so one investment can spread across several underlying funds at once.

A multi-asset fund of funds spreads money across different kinds of underlying schemes, for example equity schemes, debt schemes, and gold and silver ETFs.

Because the underlying schemes carry their own expenses, the investor bears costs at two levels. The scheme information document sets out the expense limits, the underlying funds and the risk level, so it is worth reading before investing.
