6.3 · LEARN
L-017: What Macaulay duration measures
Macaulay duration is the weighted average time, in years, that it takes to recover the price paid for a bond through the present value of its expected cash flows: its interest payments and the repayment of principal (Tata Mutual Fund).
For a debt fund it is usually shorter than the fund's average maturity, zero-coupon bonds being the exception (Tata Mutual Fund). SEBI's categories use the portfolio's Macaulay duration: a short duration fund, for example, keeps it between one and three years (AMFI).
A fund with a higher Macaulay duration is more sensitive to changes in interest rates. A related measure, modified duration, approximates the percentage change in a fund's NAV for a one percentage point change in rates (Tata Mutual Fund).
