2.1 · WHAT IS DRIVING MARKETS
Foreign buying and lower bond yields set the tone; Diwali brings a special session and a holiday
- Foreign flows: Foreign investors bought a net ₹14,975 crore on 5 Nov (provisional), after ₹7,284 crore on 4 Nov. Foreign flows feed into large-cap share prices and the rupee; provisional figures can be revised when final data is published. (Business Standard, Upstox FII/DII data)
- Crude oil: Brent touched an intraday low of about $93.50 a barrel on 5 Nov (Upstox News), then settled 3.88% higher at $98.29 after fresh tanker attacks near the Strait of Hormuz, Reuters reported. India imports most of its crude, so oil prices reach the import bill, the rupee and inflation. (Reuters via Lufkin Daily News, Upstox News)
- The rupee and bonds: PTI said lower crude and foreign inflows lifted the rupee to 94.41 per dollar. The 10-year government bond yield fell to 7.17%, down 24 basis points so far in November, Business Standard reported. Bond prices rise when yields fall. (PTI via ThePrint, Business Standard)
- Inflation: Retail inflation was 5.54% in September, up from 4.83% in August (Business Today). October's reading is due on 12 Nov, according to MoSPI's release calendar. (Business Today, MoSPI advance release calendar)

2.2 · HOW GLOBAL MARKETS REACH INDIA
- US shares: The Nasdaq fell 5.52%, its worst day since April 2025, while the S&P 500 lost 4.36% and the Dow 3.44% on 5 Nov as chip and AI shares were sold (Reuters). Sharp US moves reach India through overnight sentiment, foreign flows and the US clients of Indian IT companies. (Reuters via Investing, Fox Business)
- US yields and the dollar: The US 10-year Treasury yield fell about 5 basis points to about 4.84% as investors moved into bonds, and the dollar index was little changed. US yields and the dollar shape foreign flows and the rupee. (Rio Times, AP via NBC Bay Area)
- Asian markets: Asian shares fell in early trade on 6 Nov, Reuters reported: the Kospi dropped more than 8%, setting off a trading halt, and the Nikkei was about 6% lower. Asia is the last major cue before the Indian open. (Reuters via MarketScreener)

2.3 · EVENTS AND DATA AHEAD

- Folios rose 0.9% to 28.62 crore, from 28.35 crore in August. (AMFI Monthly Note, September 2026)

Know your categories, Mid Cap Fund: Under SEBI's categorisation rules, a mid cap fund is an open-ended equity scheme that must keep at least 65% of its total assets in the shares of mid cap companies, which SEBI defines as the 101st to 250th listed companies by full market capitalisation. SEBI's circular of 26 February 2026 kept the 65% minimum. These are equity schemes, so their value rises and falls with the share prices of mid-sized companies, and each scheme's riskometer level is set out in the fund house's own documents. (SEBI, AMFI (copy of SEBI circular of 6 Oct 2017), Upstox News)

5.3 · LEARN
L-007: Where a scheme's riskometer is published
Every mutual fund scheme carries a riskometer, a dial that shows the level of risk of the scheme's portfolio, from low to very high.
For a new fund offer, the riskometer appears in the scheme's key information memorandum and scheme information document, which the fund house publishes before the offer opens.
The level describes the portfolio, not the investor, and it can change after launch as the portfolio changes, so the fund house's latest documents carry the current level.
