6.3 · LEARN

L-018: What a credit rating such as AA+ tells you

A credit rating, such as AAA, AA+ or AA−, is a symbol that rating agencies such as CRISIL and CARE give a borrower's bonds. It sums up the borrower's creditworthiness: its ability to repay interest and principal (DSP Mutual Fund).

Higher ratings indicate relatively higher safety, and such borrowers usually pay lower interest. Even a high rating does not rule out a default, and a rating can be upgraded or downgraded as the borrower's position changes (DSP Mutual Fund).

SEBI's categories use ratings: a corporate bond fund must invest at least 80% of its assets in corporate bonds rated AA+ and above (AMFI). A fund's factsheet shows how its holdings are spread across rating levels.
